01

Why we exist

To help people find their place in Central Massachusetts, and to handle the move as if it were our own.

A move is never only about a house. It’s about a street, a town, a commute, a season of life. Most real estate websites flatten all of that into a grid of photos and a price filter, then hand the visitor to whoever answers the lead first.

The Oberdorfer Team exists to do the opposite: to read the ground with people, slowly and specifically, and to stay personally responsible from the first conversation to the closing table. The brand has one job: to make that difference visible before anyone calls.

Find your place. We’ll handle the rest, personally.

03

Who we’re talking to

Four kinds of visitor, often the same person at different moments.

Buyers

Want to find homes fast, then understand them. Need: excellent search, honest context, a person to tour with.

Hear: “Here’s what this house is like, and what it will take.”

Sellers

Want a price they can trust and a plan they can see. Need: a real pricing conversation, preparation help, calm negotiation.

Hear: “Here’s our reasoning. You decide.”

Relocators

Know the region by name only. Need: orientation by commute, space and street character, without demographic steering.

Hear: “Let’s see three towns in one afternoon.”

Explorers

Not moving this year. Need: content worth reading, a reason to come back, no pressure.

Hear: “Here’s something you didn’t know about where you live.”

Voice & tone

Write like a knowledgeable neighbor who happens to be very good at this.

Principles

  • ConcreteName the street, the room, the hour. “The kitchen gets light until four” beats “sun-drenched.”
  • ShortSentences that can be said out loud. One idea each.
  • SignedOpinions carry a name. Facts carry a source.
  • UnhurriedNo countdowns, no “act now,” no exclamation marks in calls to action.
  • Plain about moneyPrices, fees and timelines stated clearly, early.

Tone by moment

  • Property storyObservational and warm; written after walking the house; signed. Clearly separate from data.
  • Town pageSpecific and even-handed. Housing, places, getting around. No rankings, no “best,” no demographics.

The mark

Three contour rings of one small hill, its summit set to the northwest. An “O” for Oberdorfer, and a place on a map.

Construction · 48-unit grid · stroke 2.9u · rings offset toward NW · red dot marks the summit

The rings are drawn from the same family of curves as the site’s terrain, so the mark and the map feel like one hand. The innermost ring is offset toward the upper left: the hill is steeper on its southeast side, the way glacial drumlins in this region are.

Never redraw the rings, fill them, or add a pin, roof or key. At small sizes (below 20px) use the favicon, which sets the mark in paper on a forest square.

Primary · horizontal with attribution
Reversed · on forest only

Clear space & size

  • Clear spaceThe height of the mark’s middle ring on every side. Nothing enters it, including the brokerage line.
  • MinimumHorizontal lockup 160px wide on screen, 38mm in print. Attribution line never below 8px.
  • Wordmark“The” and “Team” in Besley italic; “Oberdorfer” in Besley ExtraBold. Never retype it; use the supplied SVGs.

08

Brokerage attribution

The team leads; the brokerage is named clearly, consistently and one step down. Never omitted, never dominant.

Every surface carries an attribution set by context, from a three-word line in the header to the full disclosure in the footer. All wording lives in one settings file (data/settings.json, a WordPress options page in production), so compliance language can change without touching templates, and a future change of brokerage means editing settings, not rebuilding the site.

Rule of thumb: the brokerage name is set in the sans at label size or body size, never in the serif display, never larger than the team name, and never with a logo unless the brokerage requires one.

Rules

Forest is a field, not an accent: it owns whole bands. Route red appears once per view as the primary action, and on selected states. Contour brown is linework only; when it must be text, use contour-ink. Water blue is for links and information, never for buttons.

Colors are authored in OKLCH so lightness steps are perceptually even; hex values are fallbacks for print and email.

Photography

Documentary, observational, warm. Three categories, each with its own frame color in layouts until real photography arrives.

Property

Interiors, exteriors, details, materials, light. Level verticals, available light, rooms that look lived in, not staged to sterility.

Place

Streets, centers, parks, water, stone walls, the drive. Shot in specific seasons and hours, named in captions.

People

The team at work: mid-sentence, walking, pointing at a detail. Clients from behind or in hands-and-paper details unless they’ve agreed otherwise.

Never

People holding keys. Handshakes. Arms-crossed portraits. Stock families on lawns. Drone shots with lens flare. Twilight-sky replacements. HDR halos.

Always

Natural light, real seasons, true color. Captions that say where and when. One decisive frame over five similar ones.

Until the shoot happens, every image slot on the site is an art-directed frame: a tinted plate carrying its code, its aspect ratio and a one-line brief. The frames are the shot list. Hand this chapter to the photographer.

The sheet

The system’s signature. Every page is a sheet: a neatline frames the ground, a collar carries the credits, a legend explains the choices, and adjoining sheets lead next door.

HoldenWorcesterShrewsburyAuburnMillburyGraftonLake Quinsigamond

Terrain is generated (tools/terrain.mjs, seed 1874): illustrative, shaped to echo the region: higher ground toward Holden, drumlin hills around Worcester, the Lake Quinsigamond trough and the Blackstone valley. It is never presented as survey data.

  • Neatline1.5px ink rule around the main sheet, with coordinate ticks at the corners on wide screens.
  • CollarHeader and footer sit outside the neatline, like a sheet’s margins. Credits, brokerage and legal live here.
  • ContoursMinor lines at 42% opacity, index lines (every fifth) heavier. Quiet on content, full on openings.
  • LegendChoices are legend entries: a symbol, a name, a note. Intents on the homepage, attributes in discovery, filters in search.
  • Adjoining sheetsTown pages name their neighbors at the N/E/S/W edges; each name links to that town.
  • LetteringTowns in spaced roman caps with a paper halo; water in blue italic.

Layout & grid

Twelve columns inside the neatline; the collar is the margin.

12 columns · gutter clamp(16px, 2.2vw, 32px) · collar clamp(10px, 2.6vw, 40px) · max content 1520px · reading measure 66ch

Rhythm

  • Sectionsclamp(4rem, 6vw, 8rem) vertical padding; more space above a heading than below it.
  • Section headsHeading across seven columns, a supporting note across the last five. No kicker labels above headings.
  • PaceAlternate paper, forest and woodland fields; a dense table earns a quiet frame next.

Edges, not cards

  • Shared bordersGroups share rules like a sheet index, rather than floating as rounded cards.
  • Corners2px radius. Paper has corners.
  • DepthOnly for things lifted off the sheet: the town preview, dialogs, the map-toggle button.

15

Motion

One authored moment per surface. Motion reveals the map and the place; it never decorates.

  • Contour draw-inOpenings draw their terrain once on load: minor lines over 1.8s, index lines slightly after, water last. Exponential ease-out.
  • Town liftHover or focus a town and its hill darkens through a widening clip circle (700ms) while a preview of the place appears beside it.
  • Frame revealPhotographs open top-down like a print pulled from a tray (1.1s), once, as they enter.
  • Sheet transitionsCross-document view transitions between pages; listing hero frames carry their own transition name.
  • Reduced motionEverything above is skipped; content is visible by default and never waits for an animation.

Never: bounce, scroll hijacking, parallax, staggered fade-ins on every section.

16

Accessibility & fairness

WCAG 2.2 AA as a floor, and fair-housing language as a rule.

  • ContrastBody text ≥ 4.5:1; ink-faint and contour only for large type and marks.
  • Focus2.5px water-blue outline with 3px offset; paper on dark fields. Never removed.
  • Targets44px minimum for controls; 40px for compact chips with spacing.
  • KeyboardJourneys and filters are real tabs and inputs; dialogs are native, trap focus and close on Escape.
  • Screen readersPhoto frames announce their brief; map pins are links with full names; results counts are live regions.
  • Motionprefers-reduced-motion removes every animation.

Never

“Family-friendly,” “safe neighborhood,” “young professionals,” “great schools” as a selling point, or any description of who lives somewhere.

Instead

Describe housing, lots, distances, transit, parks and places. Let people judge fit for themselves, and offer to answer specific questions.

We are committed to the letter and spirit of the federal Fair Housing Act and Massachusetts fair housing law. Our neighborhood information describes places and housing, never who lives there.

Content model

Structured content and relationships, so a new agent or town is an entry, not a page build.

Agent

CPT agent · /agents/{slug}/
  • name, role, license, portrait (+ brief)
  • intro, bio, quote, specialties, credentials
  • phone, email, social
  • → areas (Community, many)
  • → listings (by agent ID from IDX)
  • → articles (author)

Community

CPT community · /communities/{slug}/
  • lede, overview, housing types, places
  • getting around, adjoining towns
  • attributes (neutral taxonomy)
  • sheet position (x, y) for maps
  • → agents, guides, IDX saved query

Property

IDX normalized record · /homes/{slug}/
  • MLS fields displayed as received
  • optional Story (team-written, signed)
  • photos with briefs until shot
  • → agent, community
  • vendor adapter: any IDX feed

Guide

post type post · category taxonomy
  • title, dek, body blocks, photo
  • author → Agent
  • → communities (many)

Market update

CPT market_update
  • period, community, figures with source + date
  • no figure publishes without a source

Brokerage settings

options page
  • legal name, address, license notes
  • attribution strings by context
  • IDX disclaimer, fair-housing text
  • swap affiliation without a rebuild

Lead routing metadata

Every form sends the same shape to Follow Up Boss: person (name, email, phone, tags), type (Property / Seller / General Inquiry), message, property (address, MLS #, price, URL), campaign (UTM, first touch), page URL, and a routing block: agent (from an agent page, a listing’s agent, or the visitor’s choice), community and intent. Agent-level attribution; team-level rules decide who replies.
